- `subtitles.mkv` (24 KB): seven seconds of test pattern with English mono AAC and eight English subtitle tracks, each with cues at times of its own, for `SubtitleCuesDeviceTest` and `PlaySubtitleFormatsTest`. Track 3 is PGS, white boxes of 40x10, 48x10 and 56x10 at 1-2, 3-4 and 5-6 s; 4 is PGS flagged forced, 24x12 and 32x12 at 1.5-2.5 and 4.5-5.5 s; 5 is SRT, "One", "Two" and "Three" from 1.2, 3.2 and 5.2 s; 6 is ASS, "Alpha", "Beta" and "Gamma" from 1.4, 3.4 and 5.4 s; 7 is VobSub, boxes of 64x12, 72x12 and 80x12 from 1.6, 3.6 and 5.6 s. Each of those text cues lasts a second; a VobSub cue's stop comes 87 ticks of 1024/90,000 s after its start (0.99 s), which Media3 reads as hundredths of a second, so it shows for 0.87 s. Tracks 8 and 9 have cues that overlap: 8 is ASS, a sign "North Gate" (its own style, placed with `\pos`) at 1-4 s with "Which way now?" at 2-3 s inside it, then "First voice" and "Second voice" both at 5-6 s; 9 is SRT, "[Rain on the roof]" at 1-4 s with "Close the window." at 2-3 s inside it. Track 10 is DVB, boxes of 44x10, 52x10 and 60x10 at 1.8-2.8, 3.8-4.8 and 5.8-6.8 s. ffmpeg can't encode PGS, so `tools/pgs/make_sup.py` writes the .sup files; ffmpeg turns one into VobSub (dvdsub) and one into DVB. Media3 reads a DVB track in Matroska only with its page ids in CodecPrivate, which ffmpeg's DVB encoder leaves out, so the track goes through an MPEG-TS first: ffmpeg's TS reader gives it its ids. `-copyts` keeps the cues' times, which ffmpeg would otherwise move to 0 for each input.
